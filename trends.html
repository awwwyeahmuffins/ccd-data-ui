What exactly is being compared?

Texas staggers its ballot, so no office runs in both 2022 and 2024 — the midterm elects the Governor, Lieutenant Governor and Attorney General, while the presidential year elects the President and a US Senator. Even the high courts alternate seats. There is no single race to follow across the two years.

So each year's number is a composite: a precinct's average two-party margin across every contest that reached the whole county's ballot that year. Averaging the full ticket cancels the effect of any one candidate being unusually popular or unpopular, leaving the precinct's own movement. Third-party votes are left out of both sides.

Two honest caveats. 2022 was a midterm and 2024 a presidential year, and a presidential electorate is both larger and different — so part of any movement is who turned out, not who changed their mind. And because these are different offices, a precinct's composite is not a like-for-like rerun of the same contest.
