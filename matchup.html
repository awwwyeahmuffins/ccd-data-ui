Matchup projector

See how a November 2026 head-to-head between the two parties' primary winners could look, precinct by precinct — then stress-test it with turnout and persuasion scenarios.

This is a projection, not a prediction. It re-plays the selected baseline election's two-party vote in every precinct with the 2026 nominees' names on it, then applies your scenario. March primary leaders may not be the final nominees — Texas primaries require a majority, and close races go to a May runoff — so you can pick any candidate from each primary's field below.

Candidates are listed with their March 2026 primary votes; each party's primary leader is pre-selected.

  • Anchor: each precinct starts from the baseline election's two-party vote; third-party votes are set aside and reported below the headline.
  • Non-voter party mix: extra turnout is drawn from each precinct's modeled party universe in proportion to registration.
  • Persuasion: flip percentages apply uniformly across precincts.
  • Order: turnout adjustment first, then persuasion.
