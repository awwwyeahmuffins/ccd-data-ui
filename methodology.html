How It Works

Where the numbers come from, what the maps mean, and what this site can and cannot tell you — in plain language.

Where the data comes from

Every result on this site is a real, officially reported election result. Nothing is estimated after the fact, and nothing is ever made up. The numbers arrive by two routes:

  • Full-ballot counties (175): precinct results from OpenElections are checked, race by race, against the official state canvass before they are allowed in. If a county's numbers do not match the official record exactly, they are rejected.
  • Statewide-race counties (79): where the full ballot could not be verified, the site carries the ten statewide 2020 races from VEST, a university dataset built directly from official precinct returns.

Precinct borders come from the Texas Legislative Council and are matched to the year of each election, because borders get redrawn over time.

How to read the map

  • Lean shows which party each precinct usually favors, based on several past elections together.
  • Margin shows how close the vote was — the gap between first and second place.
  • Diversity shows the share of residents who are not non-Hispanic white, from Census data.

Past results describe habits, not guarantees. A precinct that leaned one way last time can vote differently next time — that is exactly why close precincts are worth attention.

What this site cannot tell you

  • Some counties show fewer races. The 79 statewide-race counties carry only the ten 2020 statewide contests. Local races there are simply not shown rather than guessed at.
  • Blank means blank. When a number is not in the official record — for example, ballots cast in some counties — the site shows N/A instead of an estimate.
  • Demographics are area estimates. Census figures are matched to precinct shapes by area, so small precincts can inherit slightly blurred numbers.
  • Nothing here predicts individual people. Everything is about places — precincts — never about how any one person voted, which is secret by law.

Your privacy

  • The site keeps no account of what you look at. There is no analytics tracker and no advertising.
  • Your two on-device settings — text size and the “welcome seen” flag — are stored only in your browser.
  • If you type an address to find its precinct, that one address is sent to OpenStreetMap's public geocoder to get map coordinates. Type only an address you are comfortable looking up — a nearby cross-street works just as well.
  • “Use my location” asks your permission first, and your position is used once, on your device, to find the precinct you are standing in.
